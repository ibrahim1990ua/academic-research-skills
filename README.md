## 📘 How to Use academic-research-skills (A Quick Tour)

Let me walk you through the main sections so you feel confident from the start.

### 1. 🔍 Research Phase

Start by adding your research sources. For each source (like a journal article or book), you can enter:
- Title and author
- Year of publication
- Your personal notes and key quotes
- A link or DOI (if you have one)

The tool stores everything neatly, so you never have to search through browser tabs or messy folders again.

### 2. ✍️ Writing Phase

This is where the magic happens. The tool provides:
- A clean, distraction-free writing area for each section of your paper (introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, etc.)
- Example sentence starters and structural templates
- One-click prompting for Claude Code to help expand ideas or rephrase sentences

Just pick a section, start typing, and use the helpful prompts whenever you need a nudge.

### 3. 👀 Review Phase

After your first draft is ready, run the built-in review checklist. It asks you simple yes/no questions, such as:
- Is my main argument clear in the first paragraph?
- Are all sources properly cited?
- Do my paragraphs follow a logical sequence?

You will receive a score and a list of specific areas to improve. No guessing—clear guidance only.

### 4. 🔁 Revision Phase

Use the revision tracker to create a second, third, or fourth draft. The tool keeps each version separate, so you can always compare and go back if needed. Mark changes as “done” when you have fixed an issue, and watch your improvement over time.

### 5. 🏁 Finalization Phase

Before you submit:
- The tool checks your citations against common formats (APA, MLA, Chicago) and flags any that look wrong.
- It creates a final submission checklist, including page numbers, headings, and font consistency.
- You can export your final draft as a clean text or Word-ready file.
